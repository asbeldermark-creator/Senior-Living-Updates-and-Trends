Key Stats
49% of senior living operators report being only "somewhat ready" for AI adoption; zero report "extensive" competency. [17] Provider groups formally told HHS in March 2026 that historical underinvestment in health IT makes AI "extremely challenging, if not impossible" without data infrastructure remediation. The Signal
The industry has moved past "should we do AI?" to "we structurally can't yet." The bottleneck isn't AI software—it's the underlying data architecture. Fragmented EHRs, siloed clinical records, lack of FHIR standards, and years of IT underinvestment created a structural barrier that no amount of AI enthusiasm can shortcut. Three major initial AI use areas: HR/operations, compliance/regulatory, and sales/marketing—the last being most accessible for vendors without deep clinical data.
Operational Implications
1) Data audit prerequisite: Before evaluating any AI tool, operators must assess their current data ecosystem
2) Consolidated platform advantage: Single-platform EHR/CRM/billing operators have dramatically more AI readiness than fragmented shops
3) Sales/marketing AI first: AI-driven CRM and marketing tools don't require clinical data integration—quickest ROI path
4) Vendor FHIR compliance: API-first, FHIR-compliant architecture should be a hard requirement in all new vendor evaluations

The Crisis
The federal Medicaid funding cuts enacted in 2025 are now being operationalized in 2026—and the impact on LTC is structural, not cyclical. States facing massive federal funding shortfalls will cut optional services first, with home and community-based services (HCBS) most vulnerable. The provider tax freeze compounds the problem: states cannot use the standard supplemental reimbursement mechanism to protect SNF rates. For AL operators with Medicaid waiver residents, the math changes materially in 2026.
Operational Implications
1) Payer mix reassessment: Audit Medicaid-dependent revenue today; model multiple cut scenarios
2) Private-pay acceleration: Communities with Medicaid exposure must accelerate private-pay conversion and pricing
3) HCBS diversion risk: If HCBS is cut, some residents currently in home care will need community placement—demand surge with reimbursement uncertainty
4) Revenue cycle optimization: Medicaid billing accuracy and speed become more critical as rates tighten; every billing error becomes more expensive
